CUDA, Woulda, Shoulda: Returning Exploits in a SASS-y WorldThis paper analyzes the vulnerabilities of NVIDIA CUDA-enabled GPUs, demonstrating that Return-Oriented Programming (ROP) attacks can be executed on these devices, posing greater risks than previously recognized.
The rising popularity of Graphics Processing Units (GPUs) has made them attractive targets for attackers aiming to exploit vulnerabilities in applications that handle sensitive data. This paper presents a rigorous experimental analysis of input-triggered, ROP-based exploits within device-side NVIDIA CUDA code. The authors repurpose known CPU-based code-reuse attack techniques to discover that the gadget set available in CUDA code is Turing-complete, allowing for arbitrary computations, thus showing that ROP attacks on GPUs are not only feasible but also potentially more potent than previously thought. The authors evaluate existing device-side mitigations, such as stack canaries and Address Space Layout Randomization (ASLR), finding them to be less secure than their CPU counterparts. They emphasize the urgent need for robust protection mechanisms tailored to the unique architectures of GPUs. The study concludes by calling for actions from both the GPU industry and academic community to develop improved security measures against the expressive attacks demonstrated in their findings.
  • Future work should focus on developing tools that automatically chain CUDA ROP gadgets to achieve the attacker's desired outcome.
  • Evaluating GPUs from various manufacturers and other types of auxiliary processing units (XPUs) is necessary as they may share similar vulnerabilities.
  • Research should assess the viability of new protection mechanisms such as Control-Flow Integrity (CFI) and Code-Pointer Integrity (CPI) against ROP attacks.

Diagnosing and Neutralizing Address-Sensitive Behavior in Multi-Variant Execution SystemsThis paper explores the challenges posed by Address-Sensitive Behavior (ASB) in Multi-Variant Execution (MVX) systems, which run multiple variants of a program to enhance security against memory corruption exploits.
The authors investigate Address-Sensitive Behavior (ASB) within Multi-Variant Execution (MVX) systems, which are designed to mitigate memory corruption vulnerabilities by running diversified variants of the same program in parallel. ASB presents a significant hurdle as it causes benign programs to behave differently based on their memory layout, which can lead to false positives in detecting malicious behavior. The paper characterizes and quantifies ASB across various real-world software, revealing its impact on the effectiveness of data layout diversification within MVX systems, especially against data-only attacks. To tackle these challenges, the authors introduce a novel technique called variant dining, which aids in diagnosing and potentially neutralizing ASB by automatically identifying address-related data across diversified variants. The implementation of this technique within an MVX framework, known as ReMon, allows for improved detection and handling of benign divergences that could be mistaken for attacks. The study concludes with a discussion on the implications of ASB for broader software applications and suggests that addressing these behaviors could enhance the reliability and security of diverse software systems.
  • Future work could explore the inherent parallelism of MVX systems to improve efficiency in generating variant diagnostics.
  • Investigating asynchronous scanning techniques from garbage collection could provide additional solutions for optimizing variant diagnostics.

Mazu: A Zero Trust Architecture for Service Mesh Control PlanesThis paper presents Mazu, a system designed to eliminate trust in service mesh control planes by replacing the local certificate authority with an unprivileged principal, thereby enhancing microservice security through zero trust networking.
Mazu is introduced as a novel system aimed at improving the security of microservices deployed within cloud environments by utilizing a zero trust architecture. The paper discusses the inherent vulnerabilities associated with traditional service meshes, particularly focusing on the control plane's local certificate authority (CA) which, if compromised, can allow attackers to issue rogue certificates and impersonate services. By leveraging Registration-Based Encryption (RBE), Mazu replaces the CA with a key curator that does not possess secret keys, thus significantly reducing the risks associated with central trust authorities while maintaining performance and compatibility with existing microservice architectures like Istio. The authors detail their preliminary evaluations of Mazu, demonstrating that it can effectively reduce the attack surface of service meshes while adding minimal latency (0.17ms) compared to traditional mTLS-enabled Istio configurations. They also outline the design of Mazu, including its innovative approach to registration and service authentication, and discuss various security challenges that arise from this architecture. Furthermore, the paper addresses future work to enhance the system's trust assumptions in Kubernetes, indicating ongoing efforts to evolve Mazu and improve its resilience against complex security threats.

Provable Co-Owned Data Deletion with Zero-Residuals and Verifiability in Multi-Cloud EnvironmentThis paper presents a framework for secure and verifiable deletion of co-owned data in multi-cloud environments, ensuring that sensitive data remains inaccessible and is completely erased.
The rapid expansion of multi-cloud environments and collaborative data ownership has raised significant challenges in ensuring verifiable deletion of co-owned data. Current approaches primarily focus on individual ownership and often lack robust verification mechanisms, treating deletion as a black box. This paper addresses the need for a secure processing and verifiable deletion framework for shared outsourced data across various cloud providers, utilizing Hardware Security Modules (HSMs) for cryptographic key management and Secure Enclaves for on-cloud computations to prevent unauthorized exposure of sensitive data. The proposed framework integrates a Bounded Merkle Hash Tree (BMHT) to ensure bounded storage and verifiable deletion within each cloud provider, while a Global Merkle Forest (GMF) aggregates BMHT roots across providers to enable consistent global verification. The system guarantees zero-residual overwriting through a three-cycle process that obscures data patterns and prevents recoverable traces. Furthermore, the implementation emphasizes cryptographic proof generation for storage, membership, non-membership, and overwriting, ultimately ensuring that data never leaves the cloud and that all operations are verifiable and secure, addressing compliance requirements under regulations like GDPR.

Site Attestation: Browser-based Remote AttestationThis paper presents a novel browser-based site attestation mechanism that leverages confidential computing to enhance trust and security when users access websites. By integrating remote attestation capabilities directly into web browsers, the proposed method enables users to validate advanced security properties of websites.
In the paper, the authors address the limitations of trust in current web security practices, which primarily rely on HTTPS and Certificate Authorities (CAs) without providing substantial guarantees about the security of the websites themselves. They propose a solution called 'site attestation,' which utilizes confidential computing technology to enable users to verify the security properties of a website through a remote attestation process. This process allows users to assess whether their data handling policies are being respected, such as ensuring that user data is only processed during the visit and not stored or forwarded elsewhere. The implementation of site attestation involves extending the Firefox browser to support a web extension that can process Transport Layer Security (TLS) extensions. The authors detail the architecture of their solution, which includes user-defined policies for trust evaluation and the ability to visualize the attestation process. Evaluation results demonstrate that the proposed site attestation method introduces moderate overhead during connection establishment but negligible overhead once the connection is established, thus maintaining a balance between security and user experience.

hwdbg: Debugging Hardware Like SoftwareThis paper presents hwdbg, a novel hardware debugging tool that integrates software debugging concepts to enhance the debugging process for hardware designs.
The paper introduces hwdbg, an innovative hardware debugging framework that applies software debugging principles to address the unique challenges in hardware design verification. Traditional hardware debugging tools often lack flexibility and ease of use compared to software environments, which can slow down the development process and compromise security. hwdbg allows for real-time interaction with hardware, enabling users to step through the hardware design cycle, visualize waveforms, and inspect values similar to software debugging tools. This tool also facilitates reverse engineering and chip fuzzing by allowing users to inject random signals to test functionality under various conditions. hwdbg is built to enhance the debugging experience by providing capabilities like signal manipulation, automated analysis, and scripting for both passive and active debugging strategies. The core insight behind hwdbg is its synthesis into FPGA, allowing it to interact directly with the device under test in real time. This integration provides a seamless debugging experience that not only improves hardware debugging but also empowers security researchers by enabling precise signal tracing and real-time circuit manipulation. The authors conclude that hwdbg represents a significant advancement in hardware testing, offering an open-source, customizable solution that meets the needs of modern hardware development.

rustc++: Facilitating Advanced Analysis of Rust CodeThis paper presents rustc++, an enhanced Rust compiler designed to facilitate advanced analysis of Rust code by preserving and propagating metadata related to safety across compilation stages.
The paper introduces rustc++, a modified version of the Rust compiler aimed at improving the analysis of Rust programs by addressing the challenges posed by the language's unique memory safety model. Rust's memory safety is enforced through compile-time checks and an ownership model, which can complicate the integration of unsafe code with safe code. The authors propose an enhanced intermediate representation called MIR++ that retains metadata about safe and unsafe contexts during the compilation process, allowing for more precise analysis and potential optimizations in tools that handle mixed binaries. In addition to introducing MIR++, the paper discusses the implementation details and the modifications made to the Rust compiler to ensure that this metadata is propagated through multiple compilation stages, including the LLVM intermediate representation and the final binaries. The authors emphasize that the goal of rustc++ is not only to improve existing analysis tools but also to serve as an open-source foundation for future advancements in Rust analysis techniques, potentially benefiting the broader programming community dealing with safety in mixed code environments.
  • Future work includes incorporating raw pointer metadata into the rustc++ framework.
  • The authors aim to enable deserialization of MIR back into the Rust compiler pipeline.

Snort Meets Transformers: Accelerating Transformer-Based Network Traffic Classification for Real-Time PerformanceThis paper presents a novel approach that combines the efficiency of signature-based intrusion detection systems like Snort with the accuracy of transformer-based models for real-time network traffic classification.
In this research, the authors address the challenges of real-time deployment of transformer-based models in network traffic classification, which traditionally suffer from high computational costs. They propose a cascading framework that integrates the efficient signature-based detection capabilities of Snort with the powerful analysis of the ET-BERT transformer model. By leveraging the strengths of both systems, their approach allows for rapid processing of routine classifications while using the transformer model for more complex cases, effectively improving throughput without sacrificing detection accuracy. The methodology involves training the ET-BERT model to recognize traffic patterns, extracting significant byte sequences through SHAP analysis, and using these sequences to enhance Snort's signature base. During deployment, Snort handles the majority of traffic classifications, forwarding only the more complicated cases to ET-BERT. Experimental results demonstrate that this hybrid approach achieves over four times the throughput of the standalone ET-BERT model while maintaining low false positive and negative rates, showcasing its potential for practical application in cybersecurity.
