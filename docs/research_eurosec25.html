CUDA, Woulda, Shoulda Returning Exploits in a SASS-y World.pdfCUDA, Woulda, Shoulda: Returning Exploits in a SASS-y WorldJonas Roels, Adriaan Jacobs, Stijn VolckaertThis paper explores the vulnerabilities of Graphics Processing Units (GPUs) to Return-Oriented Programming (ROP) attacks, demonstrating that such exploits are more feasible and potent than previously thought.
The paper examines the rising popularity of Graphics Processing Units (GPUs) and their increasing attractiveness as targets for attackers aiming to exploit vulnerabilities, particularly in the context of memory safety issues inherited from traditional CPU programming. By conducting a rigorous experimental analysis, the authors present the first demonstration of input-triggered ROP-based exploits within device-side NVIDIA CUDA code, revealing that these exploits enable attackers to perform arbitrary computations. This significant finding underscores the urgent need for enhanced security mechanisms tailored to the unique architectures of GPUs. The research highlights the inadequacy of current mitigation strategies such as stack canaries and Address Space Layout Randomization (ASLR) in protecting against these sophisticated attacks, revealing weaknesses that make them susceptible to exploitation. The authors advocate for a call to action for the GPU industry and academic community to develop more robust protection mechanisms that address the specific vulnerabilities identified in their analysis. Overall, the paper contributes to the growing awareness of security challenges in GPU computing and emphasizes the necessity for improved defenses against advanced persistent threats.

The authors highlight the limitations of traditional vulnerability prediction methods, which rely heavily on source code, and emphasize the need for solutions capable of operating in 'black-box' scenarios, such as proprietary software or legacy systems. By employing deep learning techniques, the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance in both binary classification (identifying vulnerable vs. non-vulnerable code) and multi-class classification (specifying the type of vulnerabilities) using decompiled source code as the basis for analysis. Experimental results illustrate the effectiveness of neural decompilation, demonstrating impressive accuracy rates and significant improvements over previous techniques. The study utilizes several deep learning models, including CodeGPT and CodeBERT, achieving high F1-scores in vulnerability detection tasks. The findings suggest that neural decompilation not only provides a viable alternative to traditional methods but also enhances the accuracy of vulnerability predictions, marking a significant advancement in the field of automated security analysis.

Diagnosing and Neutralizing Address-Sensitive Behavior in Multi-Variant Execution Systems.pdfDiagnosing and Neutralizing Address-Sensitive Behavior in Multi-Variant Execution SystemsAnton Schelfhout, Adriaan Jacobs, Jonas Vinck, Stijn VolckaertThis paper investigates Address-Sensitive Behavior (ASB) in Multi-Variant Execution (MVX) systems, highlighting its adverse effects on software diversity and presenting a novel technique for diagnosing and neutralizing ASB.
The paper delves into the challenges posed by Address-Sensitive Behavior (ASB) within Multi-Variant Execution (MVX) systems, which are designed to enhance memory corruption defenses by running multiple diversified variants of the same program in parallel. Through comprehensive analysis, the authors demonstrate that ASB can cause benign programs to behave differently based on memory layout, leading to observable divergences that are indistinguishable from attack-related behaviors. This discrepancy poses a significant hurdle for the effective implementation of data diversification strategies in MVX systems, which are crucial for mitigating data-only attacks. To address this issue, the authors introduce a new technique called variant digging, which allows for the automatic identification of address-related data across diversified variants without requiring compiler support. This method enables the detection and neutralization of various classes of ASB, thereby facilitating the adoption of data layout diversification in MVX systems. The research includes an extensive characterization of ASB across real-world software, illustrating its prevalence and the limitations of existing neutralization efforts. Ultimately, the findings emphasize the need for a pragmatic approach to ASB that preserves the security advantages of MVX systems while addressing the challenges posed by benign divergences.

In the context of cloud computing, microservices have become a prevalent architecture due to their ability to facilitate the development of applications as collections of loosely coupled services. However, the control plane of service meshes, which acts as a certificate authority, constitutes a critical trust point that can be compromised, allowing attackers to issue rogue certificates and redirect traffic. The authors introduce Mazu, a novel system designed to eliminate this trust dependency by replacing the traditional certificate authority with a less privileged entity, leveraging recent advancements in registration-based encryption (RBE). The proposed architecture aims to enhance security while maintaining compatibility with existing microservice frameworks like Istio. The paper details the design and implementation of Mazu, outlining the challenges addressed by the new architecture, such as mitigating key compromise risks and re-registration attacks. Initial evaluations demonstrate that Mazu significantly reduces the attack surface associated with service mesh control planes, introducing only minimal latency overhead compared to traditional mTLS-enabled configurations. The authors also discuss future work that aims to further strengthen the system against advanced threats while continuing to enhance the performance and reliability of microservices in cloud environments.

Provable Co-Owned Data Deletion with Zero-Residuals and Verifiability in Multi-Cloud Environment.pdfProvable Co-Owned Data Deletion with Zero-Residuals and Verifiability in Multi-Cloud EnvironmentMarwan Adnan Darwish, Evangelia Anna Markatou, Georgios SmaragdakisThis paper presents a framework for secure and verifiable deletion of co-owned data in multi-cloud environments, ensuring that sensitive information is never exposed outside the cloud and is completely irrecoverable after deletion.
The rapid expansion of multi-cloud environments has raised significant challenges regarding the verifiable deletion of co-owned data. Current methods primarily address individual ownership and often lack robust verification mechanisms, treating deletion as a black box. This paper proposes a comprehensive framework designed to enable secure processing and verifiable deletion of shared data across multiple cloud providers. The system ensures that sensitive data remains within the cloud through the use of Hardware Security Modules (HSMs) for key management and secure enclaves for on-cloud processing, thereby eliminating local copies and unauthorized access. The proposed framework utilizes a Bounded Merkle Hash Tree (BMHT) for enforcing strict storage limits and verifying deletions within each cloud provider, while a Global Merkle Forest (GMF) facilitates consistent global verification across providers. The approach incorporates a zero-residual permuted overwriting technique, ensuring that deleted data is irretrievably erased. By generating cryptographic proofs for data operations, the framework provides verifiable evidence of deletion and compliance with regulations such as the European Health Data Space (EHDS), making it an essential advancement for collaborative data ownership in multi-cloud settings.

They propose a browser-based site attestation mechanism that leverages confidential computing to enable users to validate the security properties of remote sites. This remote attestation process offers assurances that data handling policies are adhered to and that the accessed site has been audited by a recognized security entity. The proposed system integrates remote attestation capabilities directly into a web browser, allowing users to define and manage their own attestation rules. This is accomplished through an extension for Firefox that enables communication during the TLS handshake to request attestation reports from the server. The results demonstrate that the site attestation can establish a connection with minimal overhead compared to traditional HTTPS while enhancing user trust and security during web interactions. The authors also evaluate the performance impact of this approach, revealing a moderate increase in latency during initial connections but negligible effects once a connection is established.

In this paper, the authors introduce rustc++, a modified version of the Rust compiler that improves the analysis capabilities of Rust code by embedding and preserving safety metadata throughout the compilation process. Rust's unique memory safety mechanisms, facilitated by the borrow checker, pose challenges in analyzing mixed binaries that contain both safe and unsafe code. The authors propose an enhanced Mid-level Intermediate Representation (MIR++) that allows for fine-grained safety tracking, thereby enabling more effective static and dynamic analyses of Rust programs. The paper details the implementation of rustc++, which incorporates additional metadata into the MIR to distinguish between safe and unsafe instructions. This metadata is propagated not only to the LLVM Intermediate Representation but also to the final binary, ensuring that external analysis tools can effectively utilize this information. Furthermore, the authors discuss the serialization of MIR into a portable JSON format for easier access by various tools, aiming to foster broader research and development in Rust code safety analysis. By doing so, rustc++ offers a significant foundation for future analysis tools and techniques that can leverage Rust’s safety properties while optimizing performance.
